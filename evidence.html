01 / FOOD & BALANCE

Familiar food. A varied day.

WHO guidance emphasises adequacy, balance, moderation and variety. Carbohydrates supply energy, protein supports body structures, and fat is essential. Fat type and quantity both matter.

ICMR–NIN’s 2024 guidelines provide an Indian food context. These principles support a varied pattern; they do not justify calling Kerala cuisine as a whole unhealthy.

02 / DIETS & SUPPORT

There is no one-size-fits-all routine.

DIETFITS randomised 609 generally healthy adults without diabetes to healthy lower-fat or lower-carbohydrate eating patterns for 12 months. The trial found no statistically significant difference in weight loss between groups. It does not answer every question about keto or establish results for Roza.

NIDDK describes individualised plans, monitoring and regular specialist support as components of successful weight-management programmes. Our coaching model takes that support principle seriously; it does not guarantee an outcome.

DIETFITS · Primary randomised trial, JAMA 2018 ↗NIDDK · Treatment and tailored programmes ↗

03 / MOVEMENT & SLEEP

The habits around the plate.

WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate activity weekly, or an equivalent amount of vigorous activity, plus major-muscle strengthening on at least two days for adults. Some activity is better than none. This is general health guidance, not a guaranteed weight-loss dose.

CDC recommends at least 7 hours of sleep at ages 18–60, 7–9 hours at 61–64, and 7–8 hours at 65+. Persistent tiredness or sleep problems deserve appropriate clinical attention. This website does not diagnose the cause or prescribe a test panel.

Walking distance = minutes ÷ 60 × selected km/h. Example: 30 minutes at 4 km/h = 2 km. Pace is an assumption. We do not estimate calories burned or how much walking would “cancel” a meal.

04 / THE NUMBERS

An estimate, with its working shown.

Body energy illustration

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation predicts resting energy expenditure. The original study included 498 healthy adults aged 19–78; it was not a Kerala-specific validation.

Resting kcal/day = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years) + constant.
Male equation: +5. Female equation: −161.

For a broad daily illustration we use FAO/WHO/UNU population activity bands: seated/light 1.40–1.69; active/moderate 1.70–1.99; vigorous 2.00–2.40. Applying these bands to predicted resting energy is an approximate model, not a personal measurement. Results are rounded to 50 kcal.

Goal weight is not used to calculate a deficit or deadline. The calculation can be skipped. We withhold it outside ages 19–78; below BMI 18.5 or at BMI 40 or above; for a proposed goal below BMI 18.5; or when professional health guidance is selected. These are conservative limits of this preview, not diagnoses.

Pregnancy, breastfeeding, eating-disorder history, kidney disease, diabetes medicines and therapeutic-diet needs require individual professional advice. Do not change medication, food restrictions or exercise limits based on this preview.

Mifflin et al. · Original equation study, 1990 ↗FAO/WHO/UNU · Human energy requirements, chapter 5 ↗NIDDK · Body Weight Planner scope and exclusions ↗

Meal nutrition illustration

The bowl builder adds public ingredient-composition data at reference recipe weights. Jeerakasala uses a generic cooked white-rice proxy; fish uses tilapia; chicken uses skinless breast. Ragi puttu hydration and cooked yields are assumptions.

Oil, coconut, curd and a spice proxy are included. Salt is not finalised, so sodium is not estimated. Carbohydrate is not displayed because source conventions are not harmonised. These estimates do not establish a finished-product “low calorie” or “high protein” claim, or superiority to a restaurant meal.

THE SCROLL STORY / METHODS

Moving numbers.
Visible assumptions.

The homepage oil, rice and protein scenes are independent teaching examples. Their differences are not cumulative, and they do not predict weight change, disease risk or a health score.

Oil: 10 g → 5 g

Our coconut-oil reference is 892 kcal/100 g (USDA FDC 171412). Oil energy changes from 89.2 to 44.6 kcal: a difference of 44.6 kcal. The example assumes all measured oil remains in and is consumed with the serving, with other ingredients held constant. It is not a tested reduction in a final Roza recipe or a restaurant comparison.

Rice: 200 g → 150 g

Cooked brown rice is modelled at 123 kcal/100 g (USDA FDC 169704). Removing 50 g changes energy by 61.5 kcal. The M2 whole meal changes from 565.42 to 503.92 kcal. Other component portions stay fixed; rice-contributed protein decreases by 1.37 g and fibre by 0.8 g. This is not a personal portion prescription.

Chicken: 100 g → 150 g

M2’s extra-chicken option scales both cooked chicken and its yogurt/oil/spice marinade by 1.5. Rice remains 150 g, vegetables 120 g and side curd 80 g. Whole-meal protein changes from 39.70 to 55.37 g; energy from 503.92 to 596.17 kcal. That is about 15.67 g additional protein and 92.25 kcal additional energy. The chicken reference is USDA FDC 171534. More protein is not a universal prescription.

Motion illustrates the idea; AI food photos do not depict exact gram weights. Intermediate values interpolate the model, not extra ordering options. FAO describes the general 9 kcal/g fat factor; this model instead uses each ingredient’s published energy value consistently. Final weights, cooked yield and retained oil still require validation.

05 / ROZA LABS

What Roza needs to earn.

A published study is not a result from our kitchen. Measured ingredients help establish repeatability; they do not by themselves prove a health outcome.

  • Record recipe version, ingredient specifications, raw-to-cooked yield and serving weight.
  • Test taste, portion satisfaction, reheating quality and consistency.
  • Validate nutrition, allergens, cross-contact controls and salt specifications.
  • Publish named professionals, qualifications and the scope of their support.
  • Track delivery reliability, customer feedback and adherence honestly.

Customer stories need consent and context. AI imagery here does not show actual staff or customers. If participation rewards are introduced, they must not depend on positive reviews or a particular health outcome.

06 / YOUR CHOICE

Your snapshot. Your decision.

Assessment answers stay in the open page’s memory. They are not automatically sent to Roza or a server, and health answers are not saved in persistent browser storage. Reloading the page clears them. “Clear my answers” removes them immediately.

Downloading creates a personal text file on your device, including any measurements you entered. Language, motion preferences and lunch-bag choices are saved in browser storage. The seven-day planner saves its habit, meal, date, time and check-ins only when you choose “Remember this week on this device.” Unticking removes that saved copy. Opening the planner from your result temporarily carries only a habit and meal preference through this browser tab; these are read once and removed. Body measurements and health-review answers are never copied to the planner. No marketing analytics are installed. GitHub Pages and Google Fonts receive normal web requests under their own policies.

Messaging is enabled only after Roza’s business contact is configured. You review the content and consent before opening it. Body measurements, activity, sleep and health/allergy-review answers are excluded from that message. You must send it yourself in WhatsApp or your email app.
